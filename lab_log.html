Round Numbers circa 2024

Behavioral Economics · Observation · Self-Experiment

“You spin my head right round, right round.”

- Flo Rida

I read Freakonomics back in October 2019, my first semester at IIT Roorkee, the undergrad hostel had a surprisingly decent collection of books in the library (despite being quite small). The book was very fascinating. It was probably the first time I was thoroughly engrossed in a non-fiction book. I grew fond of behavioural economics as a field since reading it and have devoured books by Thaler and Sunstein, Tversky and Kahneman, Eric J. Johnson and others.

I also like behavioural economics as I love it when the ideas I read about in books or listen to in podcasts pop up IRL.

Back to the story, in the gym I visit (once in a blue moon) in Setagaya, Tokyo, people are supposed to write their starting and ending times on a whiteboard. I observed that people almost always start and end with round numbers. I was subconsciously doing the same as well.

I decided to check if I wrote non-round number times like 17:37-18:07, would the next person start at 18:07 or 18:10? I conducted an experiment by noting down 15 instances each of the cases where I ended with round numbers (numbers ending in 0 or 5) and non-round numbers (numbers ending in 1, 2, 3, 4, 6, 7, 8, or 9).

I observed that when I wrote round numbers like 0 or 5 mins, median buffer time was 0 mins ie, next person started exactly when I ended. When I used non-round numbers to end the workout, the median buffer time was 3 mins, indicating that the next person started about 3 mins later than I ended (median), giving me 3 extra mins to clean up. Thus, I started using non-round numbers strategically as they gave me a little more buffer, a small “nudge” which I used to get myself more time, so to speak.

Cass Sunstein later liked my post about it, which was a nice bit of validation.

Leg Days circa 2024

Self-Experiment · Fitness · Heart Rate

I’d always heard my friends complain about leg days being torturous and I’d wonder if it’s actually that bad. I was a runner so I didn’t train legs until a few injuries forced me to start adding exercises like Bulgarian split squats to my routine.

Leg days were quite tough indeed. To check if there was a quantitative proof of leg days being challenging (average heart rate for the session served as a proxy to how difficult the session was), I thought of comparing my average heart rate during leg-day gym sessions with my average heart rate during other gym sessions. There wasn't a sophisticated statistical model behind it. I just compared the two series and performed a difference in means test.

The answer was pretty much what I wanted it to be:
Leg days are harder (or so the data tells).

I wonder how much of it is psychological with me dreading the next set of squat or split squat and heart rate rising in anticipation.

At the time I made this, I had been getting Nike commercials in my YouTube feed and they were pretty good examples of storytelling - music, setting and the dialogue. I thought of using my own data to make something similar.

Football Pitch circa 2022–2023

Photography · Time Lapse · Memory

“We are what we repeatedly do. Excellence, then, is not an act, but a habit.”

- Will Durant, summarising Aristotle

Back in the summer of 2019, when deciding which IIT to enrol into, program course structure was perhaps the most highly weighted objective I was optimizing for but another important objective was how good the football field was and Roorkee had an immaculate pitch.

After arriving on campus, back in July 2019, I remember playing Wind by Akeboshi. To this day I can't listen to Wind without being taken back to that time, that place: cycling to the pitch, smell of damp grass, listening to seniors shout/call for the ball. Dunno how time flew by and I found myself as a senior in summer of 2022 with a realisation that this was gonna be the last dance.

I loved spending time at the football pitch as I had some wonderful memories of playing here with my friends and seniors and batchmates and wanted to remember it. So I started taking a picture of it every time I visited in order to document it. Mostly I just wanted to see the resulting timelapse. Looking back at it now, it feels nostalgic in a way I hadn't really anticipated when I started. I even wrote a poem about it.
