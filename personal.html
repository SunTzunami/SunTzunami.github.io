Muses

Kobe Bryant

“Everything negative - pressure, challenges - is all an opportunity for me to rise.”

I gave JEE Mains back in Jan 2019 and had gotten a poor score, I was devastated. Around that time I came across Kobe's story and read about his struggles during the 1996 playoffs and how he responded to them (shutting himself in the gym and shooting all day), and I started treating the next few months the same way.

I worked obsessively for the next three months. I'd watch lectures while eating and revise inorganic chemistry at the gym; during the commute I'd be memorising formulas, for every waking moment, I was devoted to getting better. It paid off: I went from around the 95th percentile in January to 99.45 percentile in April 2019.

Maryam Mirzakhani

“The beauty of mathematics only shows itself to more patient followers.”

I heard about her back in 2014, I was in 8th grade and I got to know her as the first woman to win the Fields Medal. What I found inspiring about Maryam Mirzakhani wasn’t just the fact that she became the first woman to win the Fields Medal but that she dedicated herself to problems that were genuinely, ridiculously difficult.

The idea of spending a large part of your life working on something you don't fully understand yet is something I find quite courageous (a true pioneer of blue skies research).

Michael Jordan

“Limits, like fears, are often just an illusion.”

I've felt underrated at different points in my life. What I admire about Jordan is the way he seemed to turn that feeling into fuel rather than letting it become an excuse. Also, his slightly manic competitive nature is weirdly close to Vegeta in my head. But I digress.

Hobbies

Coding

I like coding because it lets me turn abstract ideas into something concrete: a product, an experiment, a simulation.

Japanese

I grew up consuming Indian, American, and Japanese media, and I am fluent in Hindi and English. So it only makes sense to get fluency in Japanese so as to read books and watch content without the crutch of subtitles/translations. Another motivation is that I think learning another language is a good way to keep the brain healthy. Working towards an eventual N2, I passed the JLPT N3 in August 2026.

Reading

Most of the books I've read in Japan have been read on my commute - the trains are packed but the books are helpful in transporting me to a different realm (flipping pages is a pain though). I enjoy reading non-fiction lately, which is funny because as a kid I was never a fan. I usually lean towards behavioral economics/business or biographies, reading Japanese short stories and manga once in a while. A lot of the experiments I’ve done started with something I stumbled across in a book.

Football / Futsal

I've played football with people from across the world (from every continent sans Antarctica), we might not know a common language but can convey our messages through the game and smiles.

Cooking

I like cooking because I get to make something I can immediately share with other people. A lot of what I cook is essentially my mother's cooking, so it is also my favourite way of sharing a little bit of home with people who didn't grow up with the same food.

Running

I like numbers. I've run roughly 4,400+ km across 900+ runs since Jan 2024, and I still keep track. I'd like to run a marathon some day, and probably an ultra too. I'm a very lazy runner though, most of my runs are <140BPM as I'm not really training for anything specific. I do run sub 3:50 min km once in a blue moon when an Eminem track, Linkin Park, or a great metal beat (Metallica, Iron Maiden.....) comes on the playlist.
